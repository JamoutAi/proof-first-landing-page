# Copy playbook

Use this for headlines, angles, the pain and dream sections, button text and the FAQ. Write in the owner's voice. These are starting formulas, not a script.

## Headlines (one emphasized phrase each)

**Angle A, outcome-first**
- "[Timeframe] from now, [the thing] *[does the outcome].*" Example: "Seven days from now, AI *knows your business.*"
- "Get [specific result] in [timeframe], *without [the dreaded part].*"
- "[Result] for [audience], *with live help every week.*"

**Angle B, pain-first (call out the audience)**
- "You didn't start your business to *do everything yourself.*"
- "Busy [audience]? *Get your evenings back.*"
- "You're the [role], the [role] and the [role]. *Let's fix that.*"

**Sell the destination, not the plane.** Describe how they feel after ("evenings back", "the one your friends ask"), not the mechanics (modules, features).

## Hero lede formula (2 or 3 sentences)
"[Mirror their situation]. In [timeframe], [I'll help you / you'll] [concrete outcome], with [support]. So you can [the desire] (and maybe [the bonus desire])."

## Fears, frustrations, dreams, desires worksheet

Fill all four before writing Angle B. Pull wording from real member posts, call transcripts and reviews where you can. The best pain lines are things members actually said.

| Fears (what they worry will happen) | Frustrations (what's annoying them now) |
|---|---|
| Falling behind competitors who figured it out | Working nights and weekends to keep up |
| Wasting money on another tool or course | Output that sounds generic or robotic |
| Looking dumb asking basic questions | Forty tabs, ten "must-have" tools |
| It's too late to start | Their marketing is always last |

| Dreams (the picture of life after) | Desires (what they want to feel or have) |
|---|---|
| A week of posts written in one sitting | Time back, evenings back |
| Replies drafted and waiting for an OK | Feeling capable, not behind |
| Being the one others ask for help | Consistent marketing without the grind |
| The business runs while they do the work they love | More clients without more hours |

**Pain card formula:** emoji + bold first-person-feeling line + one concrete detail line.
Example: 🌙 **You're working nights and weekends just to keep up.** Client work all day. Then emails, invoices and posts after dinner.

**Reassurance line after the pain:** take the blame off them. "None of that is a you problem. It's a setup problem, and it's fixable in a week."

**Right now vs. With [offer] rows:** left is the frustration in 4 to 7 words. Right is the felt outcome in 6 to 12 words.

A room." · "Your month inside [offer]" · "See it for yourself" · "The receipts" · "Hear it from them" · "Hey, I'm [Name]" · "From the rooms I speak in" · "One simple membership" · "Good questions" · "Your Day 1 is waiting"

## Button text by section (same destination every time)
These assume a program that starts with a Day 1. For other offers, swap in their real first step ("Book my first session", "Get the guide", "Save my seat").
- Hero: "Start Day 1 in [Offer] →"
- After the video: "I'm in. Start Day 1 →"
- After the dream table: "I want that week →"
- After the room or events: "Save me a seat on the next call →"
- Price: "Yes, I'm in. Start Day 1 →"
- Final: "Get my time back →" or "Count me in →"
- Sticky phone bar: "Start Day 1 →"

## Micro lines
Use only the parts that are true for this offer. "Cancel anytime", "every session is recorded" and "Day 1 starts today" are claims, not filler.
- Under the hero button: "$47/mo on [Platform] · about 20 minutes a day · cancel anytime"
- Price fine print: "Cancel anytime in two clicks. Want [upsell]? [Tier] is $X/mo and you can pick it on the next screen."
- Price headline framing: "Less than *$2 a day* to get your time back." or "Less than *one dinner out* a month."

## FAQ (the six objections)
1. "I'm already stretched thin. Do I really have time for this?" (Give minutes per day. Say sessions are recorded, if they are.)
2. "I've barely used [topic]. Is it too late or too advanced?" (Most members started at zero. Day 1 starts with the basics.)
3. "What's [platform]? Is that a second thing I'm buying?" (No. It's just where the program lives. Free login.)
4. "Do I need any paid tools?" (What's free, what's optional, and who gets paid.)
5. "Does it work with [alternative]?" (An honest answer.)
6. "Can I cancel?" (Yes, and exactly how. Add "no call to talk you out of it" only if that's true.)

## Turning version A into version B (A/B test)
Keep the structure. Change:
1. **Hero image.** Founder portrait becomes a real working photo, with badges under it.
2. **Pill, H1, lede.** Outcome becomes the audience plus their burden.
3. **Pain section.** 4 cards become 6, with explicit fears added.
4. **Add the dream table** after the pain section.
5. **Add the events calendar** and **platform screenshots** before the wall of love.
6. **Rename the wall of love** to "Busy owners, just like you. *Here's what changed.*"
7. **Change the price headline** to "$2 a day to get your time back."
8. **FAQ:** add the time objection first.
9. **Final call to action:** "Stop doing it all yourself."
10. **Analytics:** set `PAGE.variant = "B"` so its events are labeled.

## Honesty and claims
- Quotes stay verbatim, typos included. Trim only with "...". Use curly quotes in display text.
- Every number needs a source you can point to (platform page, spreadsheet, invoice).
- Round down only the counts that change often, like members ("450+"), unless the owner already gave a rounded figure. Keep ratings and review counts exact, and re-check them right before launch.
- Show five stars only when the rating rounds to 5. Tenure badges copy what the platform shows ("Member for 10 months").
- Don't print a testimonial that sells a perk the offer no longer includes (for example, a review praising a monthly 1:1 call that was discontinued).
- Leave out sensitive details (health, money trouble, family conflict, children's names or photos) unless the person shared them publicly for this purpose. Everyday context people give about themselves ("dad of three", "my kids love it") is fine.
- Credentials: say "certified" or "completed" for course certificates. Never "licensed" or "accredited" unless it's true.

## AI-sounding words to avoid (unless the owner uses them)
game-changer, unlock, unleash, elevate, seamless, dive in, delve, revolutionize, supercharge, cutting-edge, in today's fast-paced world, take it to the next level
